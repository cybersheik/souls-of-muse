Sound Made Visible — Jury Report

Sci Sound Labs (research direction of Souls of Muse) · Sci Sound Labs research team, with Meta Muse

1. Motivation

Musicians reason about sound with words for what they hear — brighter, longer, nearly in tune — but the underlying quantity is invisible at the moment it matters. This project asks whether a visual form can be tied to sound so strictly that every visible change is caused by a specific audible one: an interval ratio, a detuning, a harmonic balance, a decay time, a resonator's geometry. If so, the image becomes discussable evidence in a music session rather than decoration, and short demonstrations can carry a real question instead of a slogan.

2. Method and roles

We ran a bounded two-loop research procedure. An inner loop proposed candidate relationships (formulas supplied as data only; no generated candidate code was executed during the research) within pre-declared families and ranges. A frozen evaluator, fixed before the campaign, scored each candidate numerically and, where applicable, spectrally against synthesised audio. An outer loop reviewed outcomes and could redirect later windows — focus, ranges, assumptions — with each change recorded and applied only from a subsequent window. Both research roles were performed by muse-wonder-1.0 in separate contexts via the direct Meta API. The renderer and media-export tools were authored separately by Muse (Meta Muse), outside the research loop. The procedure is our bounded adaptation for this single campaign — 24 windows, one frozen evaluator, two research roles — inspired by Andrej Karpathy's autoresearch.

Development history is part of the evidence. A six-variant pilot (21.87–49.95 s per variant, 5 min 14.82 s total) was reclassified after review as engineering feasibility only: no objective delta, no baseline promotion, no working holdout; its note-band mapping failed to distinguish individual notes and contained a pause-handling defect. A 15-second note-aware scale (C4–C5 and back, 16 notes, 2 rests, 80 BPM, eight colours/outlines, quiet fades on rests) corrected the note/rest mapping for a known score: in software checks each note is now distinguishable and repeats reproduce exactly. Human readability of the code, and any learning benefit, remain untested. A first nature-themed run was stopped after six closed windows plus a partial seventh, repaired, and discarded from the successful count.

4. Representative output

Twenty-four square research cards (1080 × 1080) and six distinct 11-second vertical videos with synchronised audio: Lissajous intervals, beats (rate = |f1 − f2|), harmonic balance H2/H1 (H2 = second harmonic), decay τ, membrane modes, string resonance. Two polished 16-second English films present the clearest demonstrations. Slow-motion drawing is used for string/membrane oscillation and fast carriers; audio is real-time. The drawn nodal shapes are schematic renderings of the models; the numerical frequency/formula checks are what was validated, not physical spatial patterns independently. The public page preserves the complete original collection — seven catalogue groups, 17 original films (including the six early pilot films and the earlier scale version) and 24 cards — plus a separate applied piece, a 39-second seamless projection loop for live performance (18 films in total on the page). The loop is curated licensed footage, presented as an applied creative direction (edit and sequence: Sci Sound Labs), not a research result.

5. Failure handling

Three failures were preserved rather than edited away: (i) the pilot's downgrade from “experiment” to “feasibility”; (ii) the stopped first run, excluded from all successful counts; (iii) the inconclusive capillary window, published with its gap labelled. Each correction preceded any renewed claim.

6. Contributions and limits

Contributions: a reproducible sound-to-form media pipeline; a demonstrated two-loop search with genuine outer-loop redirection under a frozen evaluator (eleven changes truly applied, which supports that the method operates); exploration of ten sound and wave families — nine with numerical calibrations and held-out support, one (capillary surface waves, a fluid-surface family) inconclusive; a public catalogue in which every card carries its status. Limits: no physical measurements; no human learning, perception or engagement tests; several later windows repeated controls and progress plateaued, so 24 rounds are not 24 independent mechanisms or discoveries; any comparative advantage of the outer loop over a fixed strategy was not measured; colour/shape coding is an artificial teaching convention; live or arbitrary-audio recognition is not implemented. Pipe octave relations are approximate (end corrections), and H2/H1 alone does not determine perceived timbre.
